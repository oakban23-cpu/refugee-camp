# REFUGEE: painted icons: fishing, food and consumables (64 icons on 3 sheets)

Same game and same format as the item icons you just painted. They are in the game now and look great: see `approved_example/`. **Match that exact style and quality.**

## Style
- Rich hand-painted fantasy MMORPG icon, with real painted materials, lighting and volume, and a soft glow on rare things.
- One object per cell, centred and **large** (about 75–85% of the cell), on the same **dark navy-violet atmospheric background** as the approved item icons.
- **No frame, border or rounded corners. No text, letters or numbers. No people.**
- **Readable at 40 px.**

## Fish (cells 1–40)
There are 8 waters with 5 fish each, ordered from common to LEGENDARY. Each water has its own colour mood (river green-silver, swamp murky violet, lava orange-red, ice pale blue, deep sea dark blue with bioluminescence, desert oasis sandy gold, sky clouds white-cyan, cosmic void starry purple).
- Show each fish as a lively side view, slightly diagonal, with a little water splash or ambient glow that fits its water.
- The **rarer the fish, the more impressive** it must look. Legendary ones are mythical creatures (dragons, whales of light, naga) with strong glow.
- Fish must look like **appetising, real-looking game creatures**, not cartoon emoji.

## Other cells
- **Dishes** look delicious, served in bowls or plates or on skewers, with steam.
- **Rods** are shown diagonally, with each tier clearly richer.
- **Elixirs** are fantasy potion bottles, each a different bottle shape and colour.
- **Enhancement stones** are three tiers of the same idea (a smith whetstone with a glowing rune), each clearly more precious.

## Sheet format (same as before)
- **3 sheets**: `fishing_01.png` … `fishing_03.png`, each **1536×1024**, in a **6 × 4 grid** of exactly **256×256** cells with no gutters (`templates/`).
- Cells are numbered **1–24**, left to right and top to bottom. **(empty)** cells stay plain black.
- `reference_current_icons/` shows the current emoji in each cell.
